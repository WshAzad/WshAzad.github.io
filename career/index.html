Development Zone Policies, Industrial Agglomeration, and Household Income: Evidence from China R&R · JRS

When local governments attract firms into development zones with tax preferences rather than genuine market agglomeration, do households end up paying for the expansion of industry? This paper says yes.

Data： 2000–2017 county panel · 2,598 provincial+ zones · 4,455 county-year observations
Design： Staggered DID → CSDID · PSM · event study · Oster δ

Why the Firm Output Growth hurts Household Income: because attraction runs through tax preferences rather than market agglomeration (see above), the firm output expansion may account for fiscal spending on livelihood services — the faster output growth in the treatment counties is, the lower the household income is — there is a negative correlation between firm output expansion and per capita disposable income.
  • −1.68%*** decline in county per-capita disposable income after zone establishment; robust in CSDID (−1.96%) and PSM (−1.96%).
  • Mediator — firm output growth: zones raise above-scale enterprise output growth by +7%***; because attraction is tax-preference-led, fiscal spending flows more toward tax incentives targeting firms, compressing livelihood expenditures that benefit residents — the faster firm output grows, the lower residents' disposable income actually becomes.
  • Heterogeneity: fiscal pressure amplifies the loss (high-pressure −8.64%*** vs low-pressure −1.18%*, ≈7×; none in capital cities); the effect concentrates in 2004–06 cleanup cohorts and high-growth counties, and is not a migrant-composition artifact.

The Impact of Generative AI Exposure on Skill Diversity and Wage Inequality: Evidence from China's City–Occupation Labor Market Working Paper

Is generative AI an equalizer or a new maker of inequality? Through the lens of skill diversity, the answer is both: convergence within occupations, divergence between occupations, and selective equalization of wages.

Data： 170 cities × 99 occupations × month, 2021.01–2024.12 (~120,000 obs, online job postings)
Design： Continuous-treatment DID (ChatGPT, Dec 2022) · mediation · quantile regression
Exposure： GPT-4 occupation-level AI exposure index (PKU × Zhaopin)

Between-occupation diversity ↑ +0.172***
"Convergence within, divergence between" plus selective equalization: the Gini falls −0.041**, with compression driven by lower-end wage gains (p90–p10 gap −0.800**).
  • Convergence-within, divergence-between: within-occupation skill diversity ↓ −0.054*** while between-occupation diversity ↑ +0.172*** after ChatGPT's release.
  • Selective equalization: wage Gini ↓ −0.041**; by quantile, +0.116*** (p10) → +0.055*** (p50) → −0.040* (p90).
  • Mechanisms: general auxiliary-skill displacement mediates within-occupation convergence; occupation-specific auxiliary-skill expansion mediates between-occupation divergence (bootstrap CIs exclude zero).
  • Heterogeneity: between-occupation divergence is strongest in high-GDP cities (Q4: 0.337***); wage equalization is most visible in medium-GDP cities.
