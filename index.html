Research

Development Zone Policies, Industrial Agglomeration, and Household Income: Evidence from China R&R · JRS

Examines how China's development zone policies interact with the hukou system to produce stratified distributional outcomes. Staggered DID, PSM-DID, event study, and Honest-DID sensitivity analysis on 2000–2017 county-level panel data.

Abstract: Development zones are a hallmark of China's place-based industrial policy. This paper studies their distributional consequences: using county-level panel data and staggered difference-in-differences designs, it documents that tax-incentive-driven attraction crowds out residents' disposable income, and interprets the result through institutional stratification — development policy reproduces inequality along the hukou divide (Tilly, 1998).

Agglomeration Dividend or Cost? A Perspective from the Hukou Restrictions on Migrant Population ↗ R&R · HSSC

Identifies the net effect of population density on household consumption and shows that hukou restrictions systematically amplify its negative impact. Two-way fixed effects and IV estimation on 2003–2022 prefecture-level panel data.

Abstract: Does urban density help or hurt households? This paper estimates the net consumption effect of population density across Chinese prefectures and finds that administrative exclusion — embodied in the hukou system — converts agglomeration dividends into costs for migrant households, widening spatial inequality.

Generative AI and Skill Diversity in the Labour Market Working Paper

Uses ChatGPT's 2022 launch as a quasi-natural experiment to study how generative AI reshapes skill composition across Chinese urban labor markets, with PPMI-based skill co-occurrence measures and a city × occupation × month panel.

Abstract (in progress): Exploiting the staggered diffusion of generative AI tools across occupations, this project constructs novel PPMI-based measures of within- and between-occupation skill diversity for ~170 Chinese cities. Preliminary designs combine continuous-treatment difference-in-differences, mediation analysis, and quantile regressions (~120,000 city × occupation × month observations).

Regional Development: Both the Creator and Acceptor of Light Pollution ICM 2023 · Meritorious (Top 13%)

Modeled the regional creation and absorption of light pollution on 2011–2019 city-level panel data; responsible for framework design, variable construction, and English writing.

Abstract: Light pollution is simultaneously produced and suffered by regional development. Building on city-level panel data, this modeling paper quantifies how economically developed regions generate light pollution that spills onto neighboring areas, and received Meritorious Winner recognition in the 2023 ICM.

Proposed PhD Research

Generative AI, Skill Diversity, and Wage Inequality: A Computational Sociological Study of China's Urban Labor Markets. Exploits ChatGPT's 2022 launch as a quasi-natural experiment to investigate how generative AI restructures skill composition and wage inequality across 170 Chinese cities. Constructs novel PPMI-based skill co-occurrence measures of within- and between-occupation skill diversity, integrating economic task models with sociological theories of occupational closure and the labor process. Empirical strategy: continuous-treatment DID, mediation analysis, and quantile regression on a city × occupation × month panel (~120,000 observations; Python pipeline complete).
